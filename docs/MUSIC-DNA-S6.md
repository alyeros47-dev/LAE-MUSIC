# S6 — Amapiano & Afro-Dance

Instruction Package 22 adds five selectable profiles beneath the nonselectable
**Secular and Afrobeats / Amapiano & Afro-Dance** heading, separately after S1–S5.
Exact supplied names/descriptions are retained. The catalogue contains 100 styles:
70 Gospel and 30 Secular/Afrobeats across S1–S6.

| ID | Name | BPM | Defining identity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S6-01 | Royal Piano Groove | 110–115 | Soulful deep-house/jazz piano, rolling warm tuned log-drum phrases, pitch bends, deliberate spaces and gradual reveals |
| S6-02 | Midnight Amapiano Pressure | 112–118 | Dark club piano, heavy resonant pitched log drums alternating sparse/dense phrases and energetic percussion transitions |
| S6-03 | Township Dance Anthem | 112–120 | Bright piano stabs, bouncy short tuned log-drum motifs, rhythmic stops, township chants and instrumental dance climaxes |
| S6-04 | Sunrise Piano Celebration | 110–118 | Warm soulful piano, melodic rounded log drums, flowing shakers, uplifting harmony and joyful dance grooves |
| S6-05 | Log Drum Dance Machine | 113–120 | Leading tuned log-drum sequences, rapid pitched rolls, short glides, deliberate silences and evolving stop-start dance climaxes |

Every profile explicitly treats the tuned log drum as a musical bass instrument with
pitched rhythmic movement and a kick relationship, never an ordinary sub-bass or 808
substitute. Its role, piano character, percussion density, melody, mood, arrangement and
variation choices differ across profiles. S6-05 prioritizes leading log-drum rhythms over
busy melodies and repeats no single fixed pattern. Production instructions separate
kick/log-drum lows, piano and shakers, avoiding muddy bass, excessive compression and
distortion. These are text directions, not audio/mix verification.

Mood is internal. Voice and supported language combinations remain independent; Sunrise
Piano Celebration assigns neither a default gender nor a language. Existing English,
Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, French and combinations work independently of the Amapiano
identity. Zulu/isiZulu, Xhosa/isiXhosa and Sotho/Sesotho are not currently selectable or
verified in the shared language registry. The package's “available languages” condition
does not activate unverified capabilities; future additions need shared language and
linguistic/provider checks. No South African language is forced. Research names remain
private and excluded from public metadata and provider prompts.

`npm run test:dna -- --groups S6` passes 15 S6 tests and 7 CI-routing checks (22 total).
Tests cover exact metadata/tempos, selection/nonselectable heading, private references,
five different log-drum/piano/percussion cores, required tuned/pitched log-drum, piano
and shaker terms, fixed mood, independent voices/languages, separate S1–S6 groups,
bounded variation and stable retries. Actual lyrics-handler tests use synthetic replies
and verify one credit reservation/completion per success. Prompts fit the existing
950-character limit.

All 95 earlier profiles remain unchanged. No shared runtime, provider, voice, billing,
financial or workflow code changed. Full earlier-group, unrelated platform, database and
browser suites were not repeated. No paid generation, real audio verification, merge or
deployment occurred. Log-drum delivery, bass/mix quality, audio originality and linguistic/
cultural fidelity remain for authorized provider/listening review at final integration.
